01 about

I work on making robots work, mostly in virtual worlds.

At Argon Robotics, we build RL environments for robotics: simulations where robotics labs train their AI models. A lab puts its robot model in our sims and gets training data equal to millions of real-world deployments.

In the last two weeks, we improved π0.5 on benchmarks with zero real-world data, and ran wrench and shoelace-tying tasks zero-shot. Both were considered impossible without human data.

I'm a two-time founder. Before Argon, I was Chief of Staff to the founder of Ola, where I deployed 1,000+ robots across India for EV servicing.

We're a team of computer science graduates from IIT Delhi, led by me and my co-founder Pranay Gupta. My rule so far: make it exist first, make it good later.

Training a robot model and short on data? Email me, or find me at CoRL '26.

Recursive self-improvement for physical AI

Deepanshu Rohilla · October 2026 · 2 min read

Everything that has been digitised has been learned. Text, code, images, speech: anything that fits on a screen now has a model that gets better at it every few months.

But the digital world is a thin film over the physical one, and by our estimate it captures less than 0.001% of it. Under the film are the atoms. Every object that has to be lifted, turned, opened, stacked or carried is still moved by a hand.

If you can build something that improves itself in the physical world, you unlock realities humans can't yet imagine. It starts with a world where you don't have to lift a finger to move anything, and it ends with every atom around you being manipulated at will.

Every leap forward has come from learning beyond the limits of reality
